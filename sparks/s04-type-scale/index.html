Starlight Drive-In

A type specimen for a 1950s drive-in movie night.

Tonight's double feature

Pull in by sundown, tune your radio to 88.5, and park facing the big screen. The first picture starts when the sky is dark enough to see it, and the snack bar stays open through intermission. Flash your headlights once if the sound drops out, and somebody will come running.

First show: The Comet That Came to Supper

A small-town family finds a friendly comet in the cornfield and has to hide it from the mayor.

Second show: Beach Party on Planet Nine

Surfers, saucers, and one very confused lifeguard.

Headlights off, popcorn on. The show starts when the stars come out.

At the snack bar

  • Buttered popcorn in a striped bag
  • Hot dogs with mustard and relish
  • Root beer floats
  • Chocolate malts, two straws

Why these fonts

Headings: Righteous

I picked Righteous because it looks like the lit-up letters on a drive-in marquee: round, chunky, a bit goofy. It would be tiring in a paragraph, so I only use it big.

Body text: the phone's own system font

You're reading this in a car with the lights off, probably on a phone. The phone's own font is the one it's best at drawing small, and it doesn't have to download first.

Sizes and line height

Body text is a little bigger than normal (1.125rem) because a dim screen is harder to read. The headings go 1.5, 2, then 3rem for the title, so the title is the loudest thing on the page, like the sign out front. Lines are spaced at 1.6 so your eye doesn't lose its place in the dark.

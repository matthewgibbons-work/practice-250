Home Computers, 1977–1989

Commodore 64

Commodore Business Machines, United States, 1982. Plastic, metal, and printed circuit board.

For many families, this was the first computer in the house. It plugged into the living-room television, loaded programs from cassette tapes or floppy disks, and started up in seconds with a blinking cursor waiting for a command.

Its sound chip and color graphics made it a favorite for games, but people also used it to write letters, keep budgets, and learn to program in BASIC.

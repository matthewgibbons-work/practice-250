## 1. Who it's for

My grandpa, a retired high-school history teacher who still reads everything on paper. When I told him I was taking a Digital Humanities class he asked: "So you put old books on the internet? Libraries already did that."

## 3. Three examples

- **The Valley of the Shadow** (https://valley.lib.virginia.edu/): letters, diaries, newspapers, and census records from two counties, one Northern and one Southern, through the Civil War. It fits because you can follow the same families on both sides, which no single archive box lets you do, and Grandpa taught the Civil War for thirty years.
- **The Proceedings of the Old Bailey** (https://www.oldbaileyonline.org/): about two centuries of London criminal trials, transcribed and searchable. It fits because searching turns thousands of trials into patterns (what people were tried for, and how that changed), which is more than "putting a book online."
- **Colored Conventions Project** (https://coloredconventions.org/): records of nineteenth-century Black political conventions, brought together and transcribed with help from classes and volunteers. It fits because the people doing the work are part of the point, and it brings in records Grandpa's textbooks left out.

## 4. "Done"

- The home page's first paragraph answers Grandpa's question in under 100 words.
- Each example page names one specific thing I searched or read in that project.
- Every image has alt text that fits its purpose, and a credit line with a link.
- Every page has a skip link, a header, a nav, a main, and a footer.
- The nav shows which page you're on, with `aria-current="page"`.
- Every page passes the W3C validator and has no axe errors.
- At 320px wide nothing scrolls sideways, and at 200% zoom nothing is cut off.
- Text and links meet 4.5:1 contrast in WebAIM's checker.
- Every link opens and goes where it says.
- Grandpa reads the home page and one example by Sat Dec 5, and I write down what he says on my reflections page. If he can't by then, my roommate reads it instead.

## 5. Units

- Typography: a readable body size and line height for someone who reads on paper (all pages).
- Grid: the home page's three example previews.
- Responsive design: one column on a phone, the previews side by side when there's room.
- Color: a palette with every pair checked for contrast.
- Multimedia: credited images, and alt text on each.

## 6. One risk

The projects' images may not be free to reuse. Plan: my own screenshots, credited, or no image and a clear link.
